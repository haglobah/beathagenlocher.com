Lesswrong is currently in an interesting position:

- big (potential) demand from the world what's going on + big hope of us that this demand gets met in a way that makes the discussion better

We (I'm going to include myself here even though I'm relatively new. Basically the only point in my favor is that I had updated on AI becoming a big thing after reading about it in 2018) have predicted all of this AI stuff was about to happen, and have been the place where the discussion for it got shaped.

Now that AI is a thing, and especially over the last few weeks things have blown up, we could make it so (and it probably already is that case) that Lesswrong is in demand.

At the same time, this is now our time.
All this started ages ago with Eliezer wanting others that thought well about AI, and therefore writing up his thinking for us so that we could think well (about AI), too.

`and then trying to talk to people about it, and then struggling tremendously. This made him make it so that he could talk to other people about those insights in a productive fashion.`

And I think for a very small part of the population, we've succeeded. Instead of one Eliezer from 2006 that could think well about AI we've now got probably something like ~10000[^1] people who can think well about AI.
That is both an impressive growth rate, and an impressive number—but just looking at the current situation, we're too late.
Our time is probably close to being up, and _we're not winning_.

But at the same time, the time for using our art has come, and the biggest avenue for winning is actually public opinion forming—which supposedly works way better when people are rationalists.[^2]

So a straightforward way to solve this would be by just getting much, much more people acquainted with the readings, the community and the thinking.

Preaching to the choir here, I think we should try to grow the number of people who're good at thinking about AI to probably something like 1% of the world population in the _shortest time possible_, and I'm happy to take any marginal improvements along the way.

So, let's say we're up to the challenge. How should we tackle this?

I propose by _creating more grayspaces_ of basically any kind.

Over the last week, I contributed to one such effort (the creation of https://AGI.FYI ), but I propose we do way, way more of these.

Even more, it's probably a good idea to view our whole endeavour as a funnel towards rationality, and optimize the whole thing.

## The skills we want to teach

### 0. What are the skills of a rationalist?

(basically everything from CFAR, and the virtues, and so on. But here are some we could focus on)

- dissecting an argument, being able to call out the wrong parts/missing connections (evenness)
- noticing confusion/a flaw in their thinking, and then stopping (lightness)
- having a productive fight and then changing their own opinion
- wanting to know the truth, how things actually work
- very intuitively actually looking at how the world is
- being good at correcting/noticing your own errors

All of that, taken together, we call 'seeking the truth' and 'winning'.
